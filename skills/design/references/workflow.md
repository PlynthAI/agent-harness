# Staged visual work

Use this workflow to develop a light brief, establish or substantially change a
composition, or make a series. Keep one design owner responsible for the whole
result. The team lead assigns independent review through the existing role
model; typography, colour and motion are passes, not separate permanent agents.
A small change to an established pattern goes directly to its affected craft
method, contextual verification and final review.

## Develop the brief

Inspect the project authority, representative work and relevant approved library
resources through the design router. A request such as "make a series of
diagrams" is enough to start. The user need not supply references or name the
design techniques.

Ask proactively when an unknown would materially change the result and cannot
be inferred. Ask about the audience, what the work should explain or achieve,
where it will appear, or the intended character. Offer concrete alternatives
and a recommendation grounded in the available context. For example: "Will
these sit in an article or on presentation slides? I recommend article figures
for the guide you described." Ask the smallest useful question or short set;
do not ask the user to choose token values or write a complete brief.

Wait for a material answer before committing dependent work, while continuing
useful inspection or other independent tasks. Choose routine details yourself.
When the user delegates taste or says "you decide", choose within that scope
and state the consequential assumptions without asking again. With no reference,
choose a coherent direction from the available context and craft methods.

Keep the light brief in the assignment or existing task record: purpose,
audience where known, content, viewing context, intended reading order, governing
references if any, and consequential assumptions. Distinguish user choices,
project requirements and agent-selected direction. No new form, saved plan or
approval round is required. User instructions govern the task; project authority
supplies values, and references inform choices. Surface a material conflict
instead of silently replacing either. Library absence does not block design;
library candidates do not become approved resources through selection.

For a new or substantially changed composition, add a concise subject inventory
to that same brief. Inspect the material relevant to this task: the subject's
name and meaning, place, materials, actual output or product evidence, available
originals, numbers, rituals or hours, printed artifacts, content density and
constraints. Use only categories that help the work. Label known facts with
their evidence, supplied claims with their source, and assumptions with their
consequence. Missing material is a stated limit, never invented proof. For a
public page, connect material claims to their available proof when choosing
section order; a useful section need not be a sales claim.

State the visual idea and the inventory evidence it uses, then explain its
effect on reading order, composition, imagery or task hierarchy. Actual output,
a place, an object or a sequence can supply the organizing relationship. In an
established application, clearer task and evidence grouping can be the idea
within the existing identity; a diagram uses its actual sequence, comparison or
relationship. For a subject-led composition, check whether substituting another
subject leaves the idea unchanged; that can expose a generic idea to revisit.
A shared task pattern can still fit an application or diagram. A palette change
or generic style name alone does not establish specificity. The inventory adds
no form, quota or approval checkpoint, and small established edits retain their
direct route.

## Compose before extending

Identify the needed methods with [routing.md](routing.md), then read only those
needed for the active pass. Read known structural constraints early, including
fonts, palette, accessibility and motion. Defer detail references until their
pass; loading one does not restart the brief or the project-token lookup.

| Pass | Work | Check before moving on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition and typography | Use realistic content to establish reading order, a grid, type roles, prose measure, figure proportions, grouping and whitespace together. | Render a representative section or artifact at the intended size and relevant narrow layout. Identify what leads, what supports it, and whether the proportions make that clear. |
| Early independent review | For a new or substantially changed composition, or a new grammar repeated across a series, have a fresh reviewer inspect the representative render through `design/review`. | The lead adjudicates findings; the owner repairs accepted material concerns and the reviewer verifies them before the pattern is extended. This is an internal checkpoint, not a request for human approval. |
| Colour and detail | Refine semantic colour, contrast, optical alignment, and component or drawing detail within the working composition. | Inspect the updated render: emphasis and grouping still serve the purpose, and details remain consistent. |
| Motion, when needed | Add motion that serves the requested interaction or explanation. | Observe the running behaviour, interruption and reduced-motion equivalent where applicable. Necessary content remains available. Skip this pass when motion has no job. |
| Final review and polish | Render the complete result and send it through the existing independent review with the design lens. Repair accepted findings in batches. | Review covers the complete surface or series in context, original feedback and any affected interactions. The verdict names its coverage and remaining concerns. |

A substantial change establishes or materially alters overall reading order,
composition or a shared visual grammar. The representative must exercise the
important constraints, including density. Use the smallest set of representatives
that covers materially different compositions; do not validate only an easy
empty state before repeating a pattern with dense content.

Use the [typography reference](../app-ui/references/typography.md) to define
the roles the work needs. Semantic H1, H2 and H3 describe actual heading
structure where appropriate. Body, supporting text, captions, controls and
diagram labels have visual roles suited to their jobs. Consistency is repeated
treatment within a role, with enough distinction between roles to preserve
hierarchy. Exact values come from the project, not a universal size ladder.

Typography and composition stay coupled. Changed line lengths, wrapping or
weights can alter density and balance; colour can change emphasis. Return to
the affected earlier decision when a later pass undermines it, and refresh its
render and review coverage. Polish refines a working concept. Repeated fit
adjustments cannot rescue a composition whose reading order is wrong.

## Keep a series coherent

Share type roles, spacing conventions, colour meanings and drawing style across
the series. Choose each diagram's composition for its explanation: comparison,
sequence and relationship diagrams may have different structures. Matching
canvas dimensions or repeating the same layout is not the consistency test.

Review every final member at its intended displayed size. Embedded figures are
seen with their heading, prose and caption; slides and standalone outputs are
seen as the whole output. Check intrinsic versus displayed scale when it affects
legibility. A permitted scroll region still has a reading and comparison cost.
Use the drawing method's [post-drawing check](../drawing/references/post-drawing.md)
for rendering details; this workflow does not duplicate its checks or hooks.

## Repair against the original concern

Give the independent reviewer the original request and corrections, brief,
applicable authority, references if present, fixed candidate identity and
contextual renders. Use [design/review](../review/METHOD.md) for the visual
judgment. Keep the builder's conclusions out of the initial assessment and
make mechanical evidence available separately. A full Impeccable critique is
an explicit critique route, not an extra gate at every pass. Explicitly requested
commands retain their own contracts.

The lead adjudicates findings against the task and pixels, then returns accepted
repairs to the design owner. The same reviewer may verify those repairs; a
material recomposition requires renewed holistic coverage. A repair-list verdict
covers that list, not the entire work.

Follow the existing review route's repair limits. Stop a round that resolves
nothing and return the specific unresolved concern to the lead for a changed
approach or a material user decision. A limit or a missing reviewer is a coverage
gap, never a pass. Preserve unresolved user concerns even after favourable model
review. Keep mechanical verification, visual judgment and actual user acceptance
distinct without making the user a routine approval gate.
