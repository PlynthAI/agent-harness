# The brief

Write the brief before anything else and keep it beside the film's source. It
is short; a field that does not apply says so rather than disappearing. Where
the owner has not said something, choose a default, mark it as a default, and
move on: the storyboards will surface anything that matters.

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Message | The one sentence the viewer should leave with. If it needs two, there are two films. |
| Audience | Who watches, where, and what they already know. |
| Destination and formats | Where it plays (site, social platform, pitch, event) and the aspect ratios and lengths each needs: a master and its cut-downs. |
| Length | The master's length in seconds and the cut-downs'. |
| Sound | Silent master, music, voice or captions; who supplies the track. |
| Product material | Real product (which screens or flows, captured how), or a designed interface for an MVP or concept, stated as a design. |
| References | One or two named films and what is taken from each: pacing, type, transitions, camera. |
| Brand | The design authority, soul, profile and brand rules that bind the film; the lockup and the end frame. |
| Renderer | The package chosen and why. |
| Out of bounds | What the film must not do or claim: brand rules, unshipped features shown as shipped, invented numbers, real people without consent. |
| Delivery | Masters, cut-downs, poster frames, captions, alt text, post copy, and where the exports go. |

## Checking a brief

Read it as the owner would. Could two people build very different films from
it? Then a field is vague: the message, the audience or the references. Does it
promise something the product material cannot show? Then either the material or
the message changes before a storyboard is drawn.
