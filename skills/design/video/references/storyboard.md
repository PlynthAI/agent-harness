# Storyboards and stills

## Three directions

Each direction is a different film, not a recolour of the same one. Vary the
structure: where the product first appears, what carries the story (the
interface, a problem, a number, a person's task), and how scenes join. Each
direction states:

- a logline: the film in one sentence;
- the reference it follows and what it takes from it;
- a beat list: scene number, what is on screen, the words on screen, the camera
  and the transition out, and the time each beat holds, adding up to the brief's
  length;
- its type and motion approach in two or three lines;
- what it risks: the thing that could make it weak or off-brand.

Present the three side by side and let the owner choose. Combining directions is
the owner's call; proposing a combination is fine, doing one unasked is not.

## Stills before motion

Render one still per scene of the chosen direction, at the master's final size
and in each aspect ratio the brief needs. A still shows the composition, the
type, the colour and the product material exactly as the moving scene will
start or peak. Review the stills as a set, in order: does the sequence tell the
story without motion? Is every word legible at phone size? Does anything
contradict the brand rules or claim more than the product does?

Fix problems at this stage. Change the copy, the crop or the order here, then
re-render only the stills that changed. Motion starts only when the stills and
the timing are agreed; animating a weak frame produces a weak film more slowly.

## Keep the record

Keep the brief, the three directions, the choice and the approved stills with
the film's source. They are how a later session understands why the film looks
the way it does, and they make a re-cut or a new format cheap.
