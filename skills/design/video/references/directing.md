# Directing notes

Notes land when they name a thing on screen and a change in words a camera or an
editor would use. Vague notes ("more premium", "make it pop") get random changes.
Write each note as: where (scene or timecode), what, and the change.

## Vocabulary

- **Shot and framing:** wide, medium, close, extreme close; over-the-shoulder
  for an interface in use; crop to the element that matters.
- **Camera:** push in, pull out, pan, tilt, track, orbit, rack focus, a locked
  camera. Speed is a note too: "slow the push to 0.7x".
- **Cuts and transitions:** hard cut, match cut (on shape, position or motion),
  cut on action, J-cut and L-cut for sound, wipe, crossfade (used rarely, and
  with a reason).
- **Timing:** hold (let a frame sit a beat longer), beat, rhythm, pace, a pause
  before the payoff, ending on the action rather than after it.
- **Easing:** ease out for arrivals, ease in for exits, linear only for
  mechanical motion; overshoot and spring only where the brand allows.
- **Type on screen:** one idea per card, read time at least as long as a slow
  reader needs, set in the project's type, never below the minimum legible size
  for the smallest format.

## Faults a first render usually has

- Everything moves at once: stagger, and let one thing lead.
- Every scene uses the same transition: cut most of them hard.
- Text arrives and leaves before it can be read: hold it.
- The camera never rests: lock it for the product shot.
- The ending trails off: end on the lockup and the action, then stop.
- The product looks invented: replace it with the real or designed interface
  from the context pack.

## Rounds

One round of notes, one render, then look again at the whole film rather than
only the changed scene. When a note would change the story rather than the
execution, go back to the stills.
